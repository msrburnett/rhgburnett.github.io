Spatial methods for systems that change over time.

I’m Robyn H. Gausman-Burnett. I develop spatial methods for systems where providers, capacity, and residential geography interact, then translate them into location-based analysis for policy and program decisions. Education is the policy-rich setting for much of my current work; the methods are designed to extend to other place-based services, including public services and disaster recovery.

Applied research · CEPEducation policy

Gifted education: identification, services, and state regulations

A national look at how policy defines giftedness and how students are identified and served.

Before CEP’s involvement, I reviewed gifted-education regulations and research categories to map how policies define giftedness, identify students, and organize services. I then proposed a participatory mapping project to the Center for Educational Progress and designed and built the source-verified comparative framework and database across 51 jurisdictions. The work includes state-to-state comparisons, under-identification diagnostics, data quality checks, visualizations, and documentation of measures and assumptions.

Role: Senior Researcher

Research

Selected research

Ph.D. advisor: Laixiang Sun

My research addresses an under-specified methods problem in geographical science: comparing public-service geographies longitudinally as provider sets, capacity, and governing rules change. A fine-grained spatial lattice keeps residential support constant for those comparisons; related work develops interpretable measures for censored administrative data.

•
Working paper

Deterministic Mamdani fuzzy inference for privacy-censored data

Develops a deterministic approach for transforming exact, interval, and threshold reports into interpretable measures without probabilistic imputation. The paper examines interval encoding and benchmark calibration using school proficiency data.

Dissertation researchMissouri and Milwaukee applications

Dynamic service geographies on a fixed spatial lattice

Develops a reusable framework for representing relationships between residential places and service providers on a fixed spatial support as providers and capacity change. Missouri and Milwaukee school systems are the empirical applications. The framework is designed to extend to other place-based services, including public services and disaster recovery.
